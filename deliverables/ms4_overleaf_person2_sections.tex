\section{Data and Exploratory Data Analysis}

We used the public GSE96583 peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) single-cell RNA-seq dataset as the single benchmark for the final project. The final modeling object contains 35,682 cells in a shared 14,222-gene space, with 11,432 cells in batch 1 and 24,250 cells in batch 2. Batch 2 is further divided into 12,261 control cells and 11,989 IFN-$\beta$ stimulated cells. The prediction target is one of eight annotated PBMC cell types.

The most important exploratory result is that the dataset is meaningfully imbalanced. Common lymphocyte and monocyte classes dominate the object, whereas dendritic cells and megakaryocytes occupy a small long tail. This is why we treat macro-F1, balanced accuracy, and per-class summaries as primary metrics rather than relying on accuracy alone. We also preserve a Harmony-corrected visualization because it confirms that batch structure can be reduced for visualization without destroying the large-scale cell-type geometry.

\subsection{Shift-aware evaluation protocol}

For MS4, we use shift-aware evaluation rather than treating random train/validation/test splits as the main story. The primary benchmark holds out one batch as the target domain, giving the two transfer directions batch1$\rightarrow$batch2 and batch2$\rightarrow$batch1. We retain PCA + logistic regression and PCA + MLP as simple baselines, then compare them against a single supervised denoising autoencoder that was retained after the shift-aware MS4 comparison. The final supervised AE used for MS4 is \texttt{SupDAE-32-head-w1-noise0.05}.

The batch-transfer comparison leads to a nuanced result. The final supervised AE slightly improves macro-F1 on the harder batch1$\rightarrow$batch2 direction (0.750 versus 0.746 for PCA + LR), but PCA + LR remains stronger on batch2$\rightarrow$batch1 and therefore stays the best overall simple model by mean macro-F1 across the two primary transfer directions. In other words, the AE is competitive and informative, but it does not replace the strongest PCA baseline as the main predictive model.

\subsection{Condition-shift follow-up and uncertainty}

Inside batch 2, we also tested a secondary control$\rightarrow$stimulated and stimulated$\rightarrow$control transfer. This follow-up is asymmetric: PCA + LR is stronger on control$\rightarrow$stimulated, while the final supervised AE is stronger on stimulated$\rightarrow$control. We treat this as a useful stress test, but not as the main model-selection criterion.

The main MS4 addition beyond MS3 is uncertainty estimation on the final supervised AE using Monte Carlo dropout with 30 samples. The resulting uncertainty metrics are meaningful in both primary transfer directions. For batch1$\rightarrow$batch2, the final AE reaches a Brier score of 0.149, ECE of 0.009, and confidence-based AUROC of 0.872 for distinguishing correct from incorrect predictions. For batch2$\rightarrow$batch1, the same model reaches a Brier score of 0.117, ECE of 0.024, and confidence-based AUROC of 0.820. These values indicate that prediction confidence is informative even though the final AE is not the best overall classifier.

Overall, MS4 does not eliminate the underlying data imbalance problem. Instead, it makes that limitation explicit, keeps the baseline comparison honest under dataset shift, and adds a final AE-based uncertainty analysis that is informative even when the best simple PCA baseline remains strongest overall.
